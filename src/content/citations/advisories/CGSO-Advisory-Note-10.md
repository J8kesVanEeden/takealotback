# CGSO Advisory Note 10 — E-Commerce
## [2021] ZACGSO 10 | 1 March 2021

## Status of the Note

Published by the CGSO on 1 March 2021 and still public on SAFLII ([2021] ZACGSO 10). The CGSO has since taken all its advisory notes off its own website: its advisory-notes page (fetched 2026-11-10) says *"Advisory Notes are under review, they will be reuploaded once the review is completed."* Treat this as the Ombud's 2021 published view, not current CGSO guidance.

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

### Which CPA sections give way online

> "Certain sections or aspects of the CPA do not apply to e-commerce transations if the Electronic Communications and Transactions (ECT) Act 25 of 2002 applies to them or if the CPA section specifically refers to the ECT Act. … Excluded sections: Cooling-off period after direct marketing; Consumer's rights with respect to delivery of goods or supply of service; Disclosure of price of goods or services; Sales records; Catalogue marketing"

> "Conversely, the sections of the CPA that do not specifically say they do not apply to electronic transactions, may apply to such transactions where applicable, in addition to the ECT Act requirements."

This matches the CPA's own text: s 19(1)(b) ("This section does not apply to … a transaction if the performance of that transaction is governed by section 46 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act") and s 23(1)(b) ("… section 43 of the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act applies to that transaction").

### Pricing errors online

> "We are firmly of the view that a South African court would adopt this as being the correct position. In consequence thereof, unless a supplier's terms say otherwise, a supplier is bound by a consumer's acceptance of the offer and the advertised price."

> "Summary: A supplier can specify in its terms when an ecommerce transaction is completed, so long as this is brought to the attention of the consumer. If it does so and indicates that the contract will be formed only if the customer's order is accepted by the seller, the seller is not bound by an error in the price. If, however, the supplier does not make such a stipulation, the default position is that the agreement as well as any error in the price becomes binding upon acceptance by the consumer. This is unless there was such a large discrepancy with the usual price that a consumer ought to have realised that there was a mistake."

Takealot's T&Cs stipulate: "Your order is only complete once you have paid and received an order confirmation." On the Note's reasoning, the contract is formed at confirmation.

### Late delivery: cancel or insist

> "It is our understanding that this section [ECT s 46] does not prevent the consumer from, rather than cancelling the agreement, electing to seek specific performance of the seller's obligation to perform."
