## Succession from ASA

Verbatim, ARB "History" page (arb.org.za/About-us/History/, read 2026-11-23):

> "The ARB was founded in 2018, after the forced liquidation of the Advertising Standards Authority of South Africa (ASA). It replaced the ASA in terms of the Electronic Communications Act in administering the Code of Advertising Practice."

Verbatim, ARB "Tutorials & FAQs" page (arb.org.za/About-us/Tutorials_FAQs/, read 2026-11-23):

> "At the end of September 2018, the Advertising Standards Authority of South Africa (ASA) was ordered to close its doors and liquidation proceedings were started. The industry refused to sit by and allow self-regulation to die and immediately launched a new entity called the Advertising Regulatory Body."

The site says the ARB "Succeeded the ASA in 2018". (Until 2026-11-23 it said "October 2018", from secondary commentary; the ARB's own pages give the year and "the end of September 2018" for the ASA's closure, not a start month, so the month was dropped.) The NPC registration `NPC 2018/5288775/08` is consistent with a 2018 establishment.

## Scope of rulings

Verbatim, ARB "Tutorials & FAQs" page (read 2026-11-23):

> "These organisations are members of the ARB and as such, their members are also bound by the rulings of the ARB."

> "Does a non-member have to listen to the ARB?
> No. The ARB is a self-regulatory, membership-based entity. It makes decisions for the guidance of its members and broadcasters.
> When the ARB receives a complaint against a non-member, it will always give that company an opportunity to respond. If the company fails to respond, or indicates that it does not regard the ARB as binding, a decision will be made simply to guide the members and broadcasters."

Verbatim, ARB "History" page (read 2026-11-23):

> "The ARB will issue a decision on the advertising of non-members for the guidance of its members."

So the site's line, "Rulings bind ARB members; non-members can still be the subject of a ruling", is the ARB's own account. (Until 2026-11-23 this section was our reading, and the site said "published ruling".) For a complaint about a Takealot supplier, membership decides it: the supplier might or might not be an ARB member, and the practical force of a ruling depends on that.
