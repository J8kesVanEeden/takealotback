# Consumer Goods and Services Industry Code of Conduct, 2026
## Government Gazette No. 55719 · General Notice 4264 of 2026 · in force 1 December 2026

The Minister of Trade, Industry and Competition prescribed a new Code for the consumer goods and services industry under CPA s 82 and withdrew the 2015 Code (GG 38637, Notice 271 of 30 March 2015). The Consumer Goods and Services Ombud (CGSO) remains the accredited industry Ombud. Takealot is not named; it is an industry participant like any other online retailer.

## Why the site cites this

- From 1 December 2026 it is the primary source for the site's statement that taking part in the CGSO is compulsory, alongside the 2021 *Voltex* judgment, which was decided under the 2015 Code.
- It changes the Ombud's resolution target: the 2015 Code said "within 60 (sixty) business days of receipt by it" (cl 11.2.5); the 2026 Code says "within 60 (sixty) days from commencement of an investigation" (cl 15.2.5.1). The supplier's 15 business days to respond is unchanged (2015 cl 11.2.7.7; 2026 cl 15.2.7.7). Both clauses are quoted below.

## Verbatim extracts (PDF from cgso.org.za, opened 2026-11-17)

Notice (p. 3):

> In line with section 82(2)(b), read with section 82(5)(c) of the Act, the previously prescribed Code as published in Gazette No.38637, Notice 271 of 30 March 2015 is hereby withdrawn in its entirety upon publication of this Notice.
> This Notice will come into effect on 1 December 2026.

Clause 4.2:

> It is mandatory for all Participants above to comply with the provisions of this Code, to register with the CGSO in accordance with the procedures provided on the CGSO website from time-to-time and contribute towards the funding of the CGSO in accordance with the funding model as set out in clause 6.2.

Clause 15.2.5.1 says the CGSO shall:

> make every effort to resolve all Complaints within 60 (sixty) days from commencement of an investigation in terms of clause 15.4 failing which the CGSO shall close the complaint and refer the complaint to the NCC;

The Code gives the participant "15 (fifteen) business days" to respond once the CGSO refers a complaint to it (cl 15.2.7.7: "the Participant shall have 15 (fifteen) business days from receipt of the communication to investigate and attempt to resolve the Dispute with the Complainant or to provide the CGSO with its reasons for repudiating the Complaint").

## The CGSO's accreditation

From the joint media release of the NCC and the CGSO, 12 November 2026 (thencc.org.za/joint-media-release-from-the-national-consumer-commission-and-the-consumer-goods-and-services-ombud/, retrieved 2026-11-17):

> "Established in 2013, the CGSO is an independent and accredited alternative dispute resolution scheme as defined in the Consumer Protection Act (CPA). Its accreditation derives from section 82 of the CPA, and its dispute resolution mandate from section 70 of the CPA."

The site's NCC tier relies on this, read with CPA s 72(1)(b), for the NCC's power to refer a dispute to the CGSO.

## The 2015 Code, for comparison

GG 38637, GN R. 271 of 30 March 2015 (gov.za PDF, `gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201503/38637rg10407gon271.pdf`, read 2026-11-17; the PDF's text layer is OCR, which renders "CGSO" as "CGS()" in places).

Clause 11.2.5:

> Time Limits of Complaint Resolution: The CGSO shall make every effort to resolve all Complaints within 60 (sixty) business days of receipt by it, failing which it will refer the Complaint to the NCC or motivate to the NCC for an extension of this time limit.

Clause 11.2.7.7 (the participant's 15 business days):

> … the CGS() shall inform the Designated Officer, in writing, that a Complaint has been lodged with the CGS() and that the Participant shall have 15 (fifteen) Business Days from receipt of the communication to investigate and attempt to resolve the Dispute with the Complainant or to provide the CGSO with its reasons for repudiating the Complaint.

## Timing note

The Notice withdraws the 2015 Code "upon publication" but itself takes effect on 1 December 2026. The site therefore describes the 2015 position as applying until 30 November 2026 and the 2026 Code from 1 December 2026. The CGSO's own process page (`cgso-process-2026-05-07.md`) still showed "60 business days" on 2026-11-17.

## Scope and limits

The Code binds suppliers in the industry, not consumers. A CGSO outcome is a recommendation; an unresolved complaint goes to the National Consumer Commission.
