# Consumer Goods and Services Industry Code of Conduct, 2026
## Government Gazette No. 55719 · General Notice 4264 of 2026 · in force 1 December 2026

The Minister of Trade, Industry and Competition prescribed a new Code for the consumer goods and services industry under CPA s 82 and withdrew the 2015 Code (GG 38637, Notice 271 of 30 March 2015). The Consumer Goods and Services Ombud (CGSO) remains the accredited industry Ombud. Takealot is not named; it is an industry participant like any other online retailer.

## Why the site cites this

- It is the current primary source for the site's statement that taking part in the CGSO is compulsory, alongside the 2021 *Voltex* judgment, which was decided under the 2015 Code.
- It changes the Ombud's resolution target: the 2015 Code said 60 business days from receipt; the 2026 Code says 60 days from when the CGSO starts its investigation. The supplier's 15 business days to respond is unchanged.

## Verbatim extracts (PDF from cgso.org.za, opened 2026-11-17)

Notice (p. 3):

> In line with section 82(2)(b), read with section 82(5)(c) of the Act, the previously prescribed Code as published in Gazette No.38637, Notice 271 of 30 March 2015 is hereby withdrawn in its entirety upon publication of this Notice.
> This Notice will come into effect on 1 December 2026.

Clause 4.2:

> It is mandatory for all Participants above to comply with the provisions of this Code, to register with the CGSO in accordance with the procedures provided on the CGSO website from time-to-time and contribute towards the funding of the CGSO in accordance with the funding model as set out in clause 6.2.

Clause 15.2.5.1 says the CGSO shall:

> make every effort to resolve all Complaints within 60 (sixty) days from commencement of an investigation in terms of clause 15.4 failing which the CGSO shall close the complaint and refer the complaint to the NCC;

The Code gives the participant "15 (fifteen) business days" to respond once the CGSO refers a complaint to it.

## Timing note

The Notice withdraws the 2015 Code "upon publication" but itself takes effect on 1 December 2026. The site therefore describes the 2015 position as applying until 30 November 2026 and the 2026 Code from 1 December 2026. The CGSO's own process page (`cgso-process-2026-05-07.md`) still showed "60 business days" on 2026-11-17.

## Scope and limits

The Code binds suppliers in the industry, not consumers. A CGSO outcome is a recommendation; an unresolved complaint goes to the National Consumer Commission.
