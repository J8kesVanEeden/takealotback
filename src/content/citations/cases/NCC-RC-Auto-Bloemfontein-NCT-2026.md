# National Consumer Commission v RC Auto Bloemfontein (Pty) Ltd
## NCT/398794/2025/73(2)(b) | National Consumer Tribunal | 5 November 2026

A consumer bought a used car (a 2013 VW Amarok, para 5) from a Bloemfontein dealer and took it to Richards Bay, where it failed. The dealer insisted she bring it back to Bloemfontein, more than 700 km away, and refused a refund. The National Consumer Commission referred the matter to the Tribunal.

## Proposition cited on the site

On a defective-goods return under CPA s 56(2) the supplier pays for and carries the risk of getting the goods back. It cannot make the consumer bear the cost of returning them.

## Verbatim extracts (judgment PDF on thenct.org.za, opened 2026-11-17)

> [22] "After the complainant advised the respondent that she could not return the vehicle for financial reasons, the respondent ought to have collected the complainant's vehicle from Richards Bay or authorised its repair. … Yet the respondent expected the complainant to bring the defective vehicle from Richards Bay to Bloemfontein, a distance of more than 700 kilometres. This assertion is based on the provisions of section 56(2) of the CPA. The legislature's objective for enacting section 56(2) of the CPA would be undermined if suppliers such as the respondent are not required to collect goods alleged to be defective from consumers who cannot afford to return such goods to them. They, suppliers, are, after all, statutorily obliged to pay for and bear the risk of returning the goods."

> [19] "… in Edan Traders (Pty) Ltd v National Consumer Tribunal and Others, the court stated that the consumer bears the onus of demonstrating that there were problems, not of showing the cause of the problems."

> [5] "… The complainant alleged that on 30 June 2022, she entered into a sales agreement with the respondent to purchase a 2013 VW Amarok for R249 000.00. …"

> [7] "… Instead, the respondent asked the complainant to return the vehicle to its premises in Bloemfontein and informed her that she would have to pay for her own fuel. …"

> [8] "… The respondent, however, declined to cancel the transaction and refused to refund the complainant the purchase price. …"

Order (para 30), which also declares the contravention prohibited conduct (30.2) and makes no order as to costs (30.5):

> [30.1] "The respondent contravened section 56(2)(b) read with section 55(2)(a) to (c) of the CPA."

> [30.2] "The respondent’s contravention of section 56(2)(b) read with section 55(2)(a) to (c) of the CPA is declared prohibited conduct."

> [30.3] "The respondent must refund the complainant an amount of R244 000.00, being the amount she paid for the vehicle."

> [30.4] "The respondent must pay an administrative penalty in the amount of R100 000.00 (One Hundred Thousand Rands) within 60 business days from the date of the issuance of this order …"

> [30.5] "There is no order as to costs."

## Scope and limits

- A Tribunal decision on a vehicle sale, not a court judgment, and not about Takealot or online shopping. It shows how the Tribunal reads s 56(2); it does not bind a court.
- The collection point is the Tribunal's reasoning (para 22), not part of its order (para 30), and it is conditional: the buyer had told the dealer she could not afford the return, and the Tribunal gave repair as the alternative. The site's angle states it that way.
