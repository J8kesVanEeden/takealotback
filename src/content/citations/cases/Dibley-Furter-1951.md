# Dibley v Furter
## 1951 (4) SA 73 (C) | Cape Provincial Division | Van Zyl J

## Why this case matters

Cited in the South African law-of-sale literature for the **seller's duty to disclose known latent defects**. The site cites it only as "see also" on the aedilitian remedies. It is **not** the source of the latent-defect test: the SCA calls that the *Holmdene Brickworks* test (*Van Niekerk v FirstRand* [2025] ZASCA 187 para 16), and the site's three-part list is its own synthesis of *Holmdene* and *Van Niekerk* para 14.

## Operative effect (secondary sources; not verified against the judgment)

As summarised in the secondary literature, Van Zyl J held that in every contract of sale, the seller is obliged to disclose all known latent defects which render the *res vendita* substantially unfit for the purpose for which it is sold. The judgment draws on Pothier's *Treatise on Contract Sale*, which establishes the underlying obligation: in a contract of sale the seller is obliged in good faith to declare all that he knows about the thing sold to the purchaser.

Where this duty is breached, the buyer's aedilitian remedies are available:
- **actio redhibitoria** — cancellation and full refund;
- **actio quanti minoris** — price reduction equal to the diminution in value attributable to the defect.

## The latent-defect test (not from this case)

The site's three-part list (hidden; present at sale; material) is its own synthesis of the definition in *Holmdene Brickworks v Roberts Construction* 1977 (3) SA 670 (A) at 683H–684A, which the SCA calls the *Holmdene Brickworks* test (*Van Niekerk* para 16), and the requirements the SCA lists in *Van Niekerk* para 14. See [Holmdene-Brickworks-1977.md](./Holmdene-Brickworks-1977.md).

**Correction (2026-11-18).** An earlier version of this file called *Dibley* a "canonical SA restatement" and the "foundation of the three-part aedilitian test", said the test came "across *Dibley v Furter* and *Glaston House v Inag*", and stated Van Zyl J's holding as fact. None of that was verified against the judgment, and the SCA's 2025 restatement does not cite *Dibley*.
