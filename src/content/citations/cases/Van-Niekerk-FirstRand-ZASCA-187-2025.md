# Van Niekerk v FirstRand Bank Limited
## [2025] ZASCA 187; 2026 (2) SA 516 (SCA) | Supreme Court of Appeal | 10 December 2025

Unanimous five-judge bench (judgment by Steyn AJA). The buyer of a second-hand vehicle financed by the bank discovered latent defects, cancelled, and was sued by the bank. The SCA upheld her appeal: the cancellation was confirmed and the bank ordered to pay her R170 023.23 plus interest.

## Propositions cited on the site

1. **The modern SCA restatement of the latent-defect test.** The canonical cases the site relies on (*Phame*, *Glaston House*) are from the 1970s; this is the SCA reaffirming them in 2025.
2. **Accepting an item back after a failed repair is not a waiver** of the right to cancel for the latent defect.
3. **Goods bought on credit stay inside the CPA.** The credit agreement is governed by the National Credit Act, but CPA s 5(2)(d) keeps the goods themselves within the CPA.

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

> [16] "This Court in Glaston House (Pty) Ltd v Inag (Pty) Ltd took a broad view of what constituted a latent defect. It is now settled that any material imperfection preventing or hindering the ordinary or common use of the res vendita (the thing sold) is an aedilitian defect."

> [19] "There was no evidence that could warrant a conclusion that Mrs van Niekerk's conduct, constituted a waiver, implied or imputed by the operation of the law. The fact that the vehicle was accepted back after it was first returned to Gerrie after its purported repair did not constitute a waiver of Mrs van Niekerk's right to cancel the agreement when she and Gerrie first became aware of the latent defects. The high court overlooked the latter part of s 5(2)(d) of the CPA … The agreement between Mrs van Niekerk and the bank was excluded from the operation of the CPA, but the vehicle which was the subject matter of the agreement, was not excluded."

## Scope and limits

- The SCA expressly declined to settle the general s 69 "exhaust other forums first" question: "It is therefore not necessary to seek to resolve the position concerning that section in these proceedings" (para 27). The site does not cite it on that point.
- Vehicle on credit; the latent-defect restatement is general.
