# Van Niekerk v FirstRand Bank Limited
## [2025] ZASCA 187; 2026 (2) SA 516 (SCA) | Supreme Court of Appeal | 10 December 2025

Unanimous five-judge bench (judgment by Steyn AJA). The buyer of a second-hand vehicle financed by the bank discovered latent defects, cancelled, and was sued by the bank. The SCA upheld her appeal: the cancellation was confirmed and the bank ordered to pay her R170 023.23 plus interest.

## Propositions cited on the site

1. **The modern SCA restatement of the latent-defect test.** The SCA calls it the *Holmdene Brickworks* test (para 16) and lists the requirements of the actio redhibitoria, including that the buyer "must act within a reasonable time after discovering the defect" (para 14). It relies on *Phame* (para 13), *Glaston House* and *Holmdene Brickworks* (para 16).
2. **Accepting an item back after a failed repair is not a waiver** of the right to cancel for the latent defect.
3. **Goods bought on credit stay inside the CPA.** The credit agreement is governed by the National Credit Act, but CPA s 5(2)(d) keeps the goods themselves within the CPA.

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

> [13] "The actio redhibitoria is an aedilitian action for setting aside the contract and for restoring the position of the parties to what it was prior to them entering into the contract, so far as that is possible. In Phame (Pty) Ltd v Paizes the Court held: '(i) If there is a latent defect, at the time of the sale ipso facto the aedilitian remedy is available (unless excluded by agreement). The seller's obligation and the buyer's right arise by operation of law, and not by reference to the intention of the parties. … Nor does the buyer have to aver and prove a breach of a term of the contract…. (ii) Similarly, if during the negotiations the seller made a dictum et promissum bearing on the quality of the res vendita and it falls short of it, ipso facto the aedilitian remedy is available, by operation of law. There is no need to invoke any warranty or term or to aver the breach of either. Indeed, that is one of the reasons why the aedilitian remedy is useful to buyers…'" (footnotes: *Phame (Pty) Ltd v Paizes* 1973 (3) SA 397 (A) at 416H–417A)

> [14] "The trite requirements for succeeding with the actio redhibitoria are: the thing sold had a defect that impaired its utility or effectiveness; the defect existed at the time of the sale; the defect was latent and not visible upon inspection; the purchaser was unaware of its existence; the purchaser would not have purchased the item had she known of the defect; and she is willing and able to make restitution. Importantly, the buyer must act within a reasonable time after discovering the defect and return the item to the seller."

> [16] "It is required, briefly, to consider the nature of the defects of the vehicle, since the bank accepted that they were latent defects only for the purposes of its argument regarding the cancellation of the agreement. This Court in Glaston House (Pty) Ltd v Inag (Pty) Ltd took a broad view of what constituted a latent defect. It is now settled that any material imperfection preventing or hindering the ordinary or common use of the res vendita (the thing sold) is an aedilitian defect. Importantly, Corbett JA, as he then was, in Holmdene Brickworks (Pty) Ltd v Roberts Construction Co Ltd stated: 'Broadly speaking in this context a defect may be described as an abnormal quality or attribute which destroys or substantially impairs the utility or effectiveness of the res vendita, for the purpose for which it has been sold or for which it is commonly used… Such a defect is latent when it is one which is not visible or discoverable upon an inspection of the res vendita.' (Footnotes omitted.) In Odendaal v Ferraris, this Court referred to the test for latent defects as the Holmdene Brickworks test." (footnotes: *Glaston House* 1977 (2) SA 846 (A) at 866F; *Holmdene Brickworks* 1977 (3) SA 670 (A) at 683H–684A; *Odendaal v Ferraris* [2008] ZASCA 85 para 26)

(Paras 13, 14 and the full para 16 added 2026-11-17 from the SAFLII text, Wayback capture 20260116114017; footnote markers removed. Para 14's last sentence is the "reasonable time" requirement: prescription (3 years) is not the only time limit on cancelling.)

> [19] "There was no evidence that could warrant a conclusion that Mrs van Niekerk's conduct, constituted a waiver, implied or imputed by the operation of the law. The fact that the vehicle was accepted back after it was first returned to Gerrie after its purported repair did not constitute a waiver of Mrs van Niekerk's right to cancel the agreement when she and Gerrie first became aware of the latent defects. The high court overlooked the latter part of s 5(2)(d) of the CPA … The agreement between Mrs van Niekerk and the bank was excluded from the operation of the CPA, but the vehicle which was the subject matter of the agreement, was not excluded."

## Scope and limits

- The SCA expressly declined to settle the general s 69 "exhaust other forums first" question: "It is therefore not necessary to seek to resolve the position concerning that section in these proceedings" (para 27). The site does not cite it on that point.
- Vehicle on credit; the latent-defect restatement is general.
