# Motus Corporation (Pty) Ltd and Another v Wentzel
## [2021] ZASCA 40 | Supreme Court of Appeal | 13 April 2021

## Summary of what this judgment is relied on for

Site uses it **only** for the SCA's obiter guidance on section 69(d) of the Consumer Protection Act: that s 69(d) "should not lightly be read as excluding the right of consumers to approach the court in order to obtain redress" (para 26), and that it refers to remedies "available in terms of national legislation" rather than only those "in terms of this Act" (para 27). The SCA left the question open ("The issues arising from the section will need to be resolved on another occasion"). It did not hold that consumers may go straight to court; for a holding to that effect in the Western Cape, see *Pure Silk v Martin* [2025] ZAWCHC 568 ([`Pure-Silk-Martin-ZAWCHC-568-2025.md`](./Pure-Silk-Martin-ZAWCHC-568-2025.md)).

(An earlier version of this summary said the obiter means "consumers may approach a court directly". That overstates paras 26–27, which only say s 69(d) should not lightly be read as excluding court access, and leave the point open. Corrected 2026-11-17; quotes re-checked against the SAFLII text, Wayback capture 20261110162447.)

## The critical paragraphs: paras 26 and 27 (verbatim, checked against SAFLII 2026-11-10)

Para 26:

> "The need for us to address the scope of s 69(d) fell away in argument, because Mr Botes SC, who appeared for Renault and Renault SA, indicated that he would not pursue the point as his clients preferred to address the issues of substance. Therefore, we did not hear full argument on the matter. The issues arising from the section will need to be resolved on another occasion. It suffices to say that the primary guide in interpreting the section will be s 34 of the Constitution and the guarantee of the right of access to courts. Section 69(d) should not lightly be read as excluding the right of consumers to approach the court in order to obtain redress."

Para 27 (the "national legislation" point the site relies on):

> "Furthermore, subsec (d) does not refer to the consumer pursuing all other remedies 'in terms of this Act', but of pursuing all other remedies available in terms of national legislation."

(An earlier version of this file quoted para 26 as "the court did not hear full argument"; the judgment says "we did not hear full argument". Corrected 2026-11-10.)

This makes the SCA's reasoning about s 69(d) **obiter dicta**, not ratio. The court expressly declined to decide the point. Secondary commentary treats it the same way.

## Other holdings in the case

The SCA disposed of the appeal on section 56(3) grounds, finding that Ms Wentzel had not satisfied the s 56(3) requirements for a refund. The High Court's refund order was set aside.

## Why we still cite it

Even as obiter, the guidance is from the SCA and has been discussed in commentary:

- Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr commentary (22 June 2021): calls the s 69 discussion "remarks" made without full argument (it does not use the word "obiter"; see [cliffe-dekker-motus-2021.md](../commentary/cliffe-dekker-motus-2021.md))
- Financial Institutions Legal Snapshot (15 August 2022): treated as obiter (no copy held in this repo; not re-checked)
- ENSafrica: discussed in the "Straight shot to the courts" note, which reports that the court in *Steynberg* "cited the obiter remarks" (see [ens-straight-shot-cpa.md](../commentary/ens-straight-shot-cpa.md))
- IR Global: same obiter framing (no copy held in this repo; not re-checked)

The argument for citing Motus on the site is: *this is SCA direction, not SCA holding, but it's still the highest-court signal on the s 69(d) question and the commentary reads it the same way.*
