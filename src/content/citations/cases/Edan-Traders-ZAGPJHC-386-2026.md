# Edan Traders (Pty) Ltd v National Consumer Tribunal and Others
## [2026] ZAGPJHC 386 | Gauteng Division, Johannesburg (Full Court) | 13 April 2026

Judgment by Yacoob J, Windell and Wanless JJ concurring. Marked "REPORTABLE: YES". An appeal by a used-vehicle dealer against a National Consumer Tribunal finding of prohibited conduct under the CPA; the appeal was dismissed.

## Proposition cited on the site

In a defective-goods dispute under the CPA, **the consumer has to show that there were problems with the goods, not what caused them.** Once problems that arose shortly after delivery are shown, **the supplier carries the onus** to rebut the conclusion that the goods were defective and to prove that the problems came from the consumer's own conduct.

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

> [36] "Mr Prinsloo, who appeared for Edan, referred the court in his reply to s 117 of the CPA, which provides that the standard of proof in the Tribunal is on a balance of probabilities. This of course does not determine where the onus lies. … In that judgment [*Toyota Randburg v Ndlovu*], by a Full Bench of the Gauteng Division, the issue of onus on an applicant before the Tribunal was specifically dealt with. The court found that, where the purchaser shows that problems with the vehicle arose very shortly after delivery, the onus was on the appellant (the supplier of the vehicle) to rebut the conclusion that there was a defect, and to prove that the issues arose from the purchaser's negligence. The onus on the purchaser is to demonstrate that there were problems, not to show the cause of the problems. This is consistent too with Edan's contention that Ms Ndlovu is not an expert and could not prove what the cause of the problems were."

> [45] "… the imposition of a one month warranty seeks to avoid the obligation of a six-month warranty imposed by section 56(2). Edan's reliance on the voetstoots clause in its standard terms and conditions to the extent that it seeks to rely on it to escape the obligations imposed by the CPA. …"

## Scope and limits

- The goods were a vehicle and the forum was the National Consumer Tribunal. The onus reasoning is general: it turns on the consumer not having the technical knowledge to prove the cause of a fault, which applies with more force to a consumer returning electronics to an online retailer.
- It is a Full Court decision of the Gauteng Division (Johannesburg). It binds courts in that Division; elsewhere it is persuasive.
- It adopts and applies [*Toyota Randburg v Ndlovu* [2024] ZAGPPHC 591](./Toyota-Randburg-Ndlovu-ZAGPPHC-591-2024.md). See also [*Wingfield Motors v NCT* [2025] ZAWCHC 385](./Wingfield-Motors-ZAWCHC-385-2025.md) for the same approach in the Western Cape.
