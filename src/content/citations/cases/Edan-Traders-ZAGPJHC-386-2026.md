# Edan Traders (Pty) Ltd v National Consumer Tribunal and Others
## [2026] ZAGPJHC 386 | Gauteng Division, Johannesburg (Full Court) | 13 April 2026

Judgment by Yacoob J, Windell and Wanless JJ concurring. Marked "REPORTABLE: YES". An appeal by a used-vehicle dealer against a National Consumer Tribunal finding of prohibited conduct under the CPA; the appeal was dismissed.

## Proposition cited on the site

In a defective-goods dispute under the CPA, **the consumer has to show that there were problems with the goods, not what caused them.** Once problems that arose shortly after delivery are shown, **the supplier carries the onus** to rebut the conclusion that the goods were defective and to prove that the problems came from the consumer's own conduct.

**Whose holding this is.** The rule comes from *Toyota Randburg v Ndlovu* [2024] ZAGPPHC 591 (paras 43 and 46; see [Toyota-Randburg-Ndlovu-ZAGPPHC-591-2024.md](./Toyota-Randburg-Ndlovu-ZAGPPHC-591-2024.md)). In Edan para 36 the Full Court summarises *Toyota* and relies on it: the sentence "The onus on the purchaser is to demonstrate that there were problems, not to show the cause of the problems" carries footnote 8, "Paragraph 43", and the sentence before it footnote 7, "Paragraph 46", both pointing to *Toyota*. The court's own summary line reads: "onus on supplier of goods to show issues either did not exist at the time of delivery, or were disclosed and accepted by consumer – consistent with principles of consumer protection". On the facts the court upheld the Tribunal's "inference … that there were serious defects at the time of purchase" as "consistent with the evidence" (para 34). Cite it as: *Toyota Randburg* (2024), relied on by the Full Court in *Edan Traders* (2026).

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

> [36] "Mr Prinsloo, who appeared for Edan, referred the court in his reply to s 117 of the CPA, which provides that the standard of proof in the Tribunal is on a balance of probabilities. This of course does not determine where the onus lies. Mr Prinsloo also referred the court to an unreported judgment, Toyota Randburg (a division of Motus Group Ltd) v Ndlovu and Another, ("Toyota") which was made available to us after the hearing. In that judgment, by a Full Bench of the Gauteng Division, the issue of onus on an applicant before the Tribunal was specifically dealt with. The court found that, where the purchaser shows that problems with the vehicle arose very shortly after delivery, the onus was on the appellant (the supplier of the vehicle) to rebut the conclusion that there was a defect, and to prove that the issues arose from the purchaser's negligence. The onus on the purchaser is to demonstrate that there were problems, not to show the cause of the problems. This is consistent too with Edan's contention that Ms Ndlovu is not an expert and could not prove what the cause of the problems were."

> [45] "… the imposition of a one month warranty seeks to avoid the obligation of a six-month warranty imposed by section 56(2). Edan's reliance on the voetstoots clause in its standard terms and conditions to the extent that it seeks to rely on it to escape the obligations imposed by the CPA. …"

## Scope and limits

- The goods were a vehicle and the forum was the National Consumer Tribunal. The onus reasoning is general: it turns on the consumer not having the technical knowledge to prove the cause of a fault, which applies with more force to a consumer returning electronics to an online retailer.
- It is a Full Court decision of the Gauteng Division (Johannesburg), marked reportable. On the onus point it applies *Toyota* rather than laying down a new rule, so treat *Toyota* as the source and Edan as a Full Court that relied on it. Whether the onus passage is binding ratio in the Johannesburg Division is arguable; the site does not claim it is.

(An earlier version of this file said Edan "binds courts in that Division" on the onus point and presented the para 36 sentence as the court's own holding. Corrected 2026-11-17 after re-reading para 36 and footnotes 7–8 in the SAFLII text, Wayback capture 20261110161901, raw file raw capture kept on file.)
- It adopts and applies [*Toyota Randburg v Ndlovu* [2024] ZAGPPHC 591](./Toyota-Randburg-Ndlovu-ZAGPPHC-591-2024.md). See also [*Wingfield Motors v NCT* [2025] ZAWCHC 385](./Wingfield-Motors-ZAWCHC-385-2025.md) for the same approach in the Western Cape.
