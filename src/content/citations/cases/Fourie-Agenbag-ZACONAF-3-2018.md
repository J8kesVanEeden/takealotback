# Fourie v Agenbag Motor Group
## [2018] ZACONAF 3 | North West Consumer Affairs Court, Mmabatho | 4 May 2018

## Summary of what this case is relied on for

A dealer's voetstoots (as-is) clause was of no force or effect because no defects had been disclosed to the buyer (CPA s 55(6)), and the dealer had to refund the full price. The court's reasoning is **narrower** than a flat "voetstoots never applies" rule (see the verbatim extracts below).

**Provenance note:** `ZACONAF` is the SAFLII neutral-citation code for the **Consumer Affairs Courts** (provincial bodies; the CPA sends consumers to them "subject to the law establishing or governing that consumer court", s 69(c)(ii)). `ZANCT` is the code for the **National Consumer Tribunal**. These are two different bodies. The site previously cited Fourie under an NCT-attribution sentence, which was wrong; it was corrected on 2026-04-24 in the April 2026 accuracy review.

## Facts

- Fourie purchased a 2005 Chevrolet Spark from Agenbag for R35,000 (original R40,000 reduced).
- Three days after delivery a service found an engine problem; Fourie also testified to a noisy gearbox and an air conditioner that did not work.
- The dealership sought to rely on a voetstoots clause, including a R5,000 discount framed as a trade-off for the consumer accepting responsibility for defects.

Verbatim (SAFLII text, Wayback capture read 2026-11-17):

> Para 3: "As a second option Mr. Fourie settled for a Chevrolet Spark hatchback motor vehicle. It is a 2005 model, the mileage of which has not been disclosed. The motor vehicle had a price tag of R 40 000.00 which was after negotiations reduced to R 35 000.00. …"

> Para 4: "Having taken delivery of the motor vehicle on 03 March 2017, Mr. Fourie took it to a routine service on 06 March 2017. It was reported to Mr. Fourie that the motor vehicle had an engine problem. In his testimony Mr. Fourie added that the motor vehicle had a noisy gearbox and its air conditioner did not work. …"

> Para 5: "Amongst the documents that were presented to evidence is a waiver of Warranty and a Vehicle Sales Quality Delivery Checklist. It has the following words inscribed on it in pen:- “R 5 000.00 discount no warranty.(voetstoots).”"

> Para 17: "It was argued on behalf of Agenbag that Mr. Fourie is under the circumstances obligated to pay a further amount of R 5 000.00 to Agenbag seeing that they now have the obligation to repair the defects. … The court therefore finds that the discount of R 5 000.00 was a mere discount on the advertised price and should not have a bearing on the rights of Mr. Fourie arising from the CPA."

## Held

The defendant was ordered to reimburse the full R35,000 within 15 business days. Verbatim (SAFLII text checked 2026-11-10):

> Para 11: "The CPA[4] further provides as follows:- “ (6) subsection(2)(a) and (b) do not apply to a transaction if the consumer- (a) has been expressly informed that particular goods were offered in a specific condition; and (b) has expressly agreed to accept the goods in that condition, or knowingly acted in a manner consistent with accepting the goods in that condition.”"
>
> Footnote 4: "Id Section55(6). See also the preamble."

> Para 12: "It seems that the CPA does allow for the invocation of the Voetstoots clause by the supplier. The court is however of the view that the supplier needs to disclose all the defects that the motor vehicle suffers from."

> Para 14: "It is common cause between the parties that no defects were disclosed to Mr. Fourie when he took delivery of the motor vehicle and as such it is found that he cannot be bound by any clauses that purport to suggest that the Voetstoots clause is applicable."

> Order, para 20.1: "The voetstoots clause as contained in the contract between the parties is found to be of no force or effect for the reasons given in this judgement."

> Order, para 20.2: "The Defendant is ordered to reimburse Mr. Fourie the amount of R 35 000.00 within 15 business days of the date of this judgement."

SAFLII's headnote summary, above the judgment, also links the point to s 55(6): "defects need to be disclosed in terms of section 55(6)."

The site describes this as reaching "the same result on narrower grounds" (2026-11-10 correction; earlier copy said it "applied the same reasoning" as the Tribunal).

## Parallel authority

The National Consumer Tribunal treats voetstoots more strictly: *National Consumer Commission v Western Car Sales* [2017] ZANCT 102 para 35 (quoted in CGSO Advisory Note 11 para 4.1) and *Vonk v Willow Crest Motors CC* [2019] ZANCT 63 para 44.1. The site now cites both as parallel authorities, each correctly attributed to its own body.
