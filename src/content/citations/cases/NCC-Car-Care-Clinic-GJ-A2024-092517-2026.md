# NCC and Another v National Consumer Tribunal and Another (Car Care Clinic Wilrogate)
## Case no. A2024-092517 | Gauteng Division, Johannesburg | 15 May 2026

Judgment by Ben-Zeev AJ, Du Plessis J concurring. Marked "REPORTABLE: Yes". The Tribunal had dismissed a referral as time-barred under s 116(1). On appeal the High Court set that aside and remitted the matter.

**Source note:** at 2026-11-10 the judgment was not yet in SAFLII's ZAGPJHC 2026 index (checked). The copy relied on is the full signed judgment PDF published by a trade-newsletter site (primary_url), archived on the Wayback Machine on 2026-06-16. Replace with the SAFLII citation once published.

## Proposition cited on the site

On a **services** complaint (a botched engine replacement; the NCC found breaches of s 54(1)(b) and (c) read with s 54(2)(a)), the "cause of the complaint" for the three-year limit in CPA s 116(1)(a) was the **supplier's refusal to provide a remedy under s 54(2)**, not the work itself. The court reasoned (paras 41–42) that the same applies to a refusal of the s 56(2) remedy for defective goods; that part is reasoning by analogy, not the decision itself.

## Verbatim extracts (judgment PDF, read 2026-11-10)

> [42] "The fact that section 56(2) sets out an internal process to follow, which affords a specific right to the consumer and imposes a specific obligation on the supplier, suggests that it is an infringement of section 56(2) that gives rise to a complaint in terms of section 69 of the Act, and not the breach of section 55. …"

> [46] "… The cause for the Consumer's complaint under the Act is the Supplier's refusal to provide a remedy to the Consumer."

> [53] "The cause for complaint in this case, therefore, only arose on 13 November 2020, which means that the period under section 116(1)(a) of the Act had not lapsed when the Commission referred the matter to the Tribunal on 15 September 2023."

## Scope and limits

- s 116 governs complaints to the National Consumer Tribunal and consumer courts. Claims in the ordinary courts are governed by the Prescription Act (3 years for ordinary debts).
- Compare [*Ntoatsabone v Hisense* [2026] ZANCT 21](./Ntoatsabone-Hisense-ZANCT-21-2026.md), where a complaint was counted from the sale and held time-barred. That case concerned a misrepresentation claim; the safe advice is to act early.
