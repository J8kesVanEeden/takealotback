# Television Radio Centre (Pty) Ltd v Sony Kabushiki Kaisha
## [1987] ZASCA 32; 1987 (2) SA 994 (A) | Appellate Division | 30 March 1987

## Why this case matters

The Appellate Division authority for the **material-alteration limit** on a reseller's freedom to sell trade-marked goods. Selling genuine goods marked by the proprietor is not infringement; but goods altered enough after marking are no longer the "genuine goods" the proprietor marked, and selling them under the mark infringes.

**Statute.** The case was decided under the **Trade Marks Act 62 of 1963, s 44(1)(a)** — six years before the Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993 and its s 34(2)(d) existed. The site cites it for the principle, which sits naturally alongside today's s 34(2)(d) ("goods to which the trade mark has been applied by or with the consent of the proprietor thereof"), not as a decision on s 34(2)(d) itself.

## Facts

Television Radio Centre ("Television", the appellant) imported Sony video cassette recorders built for another PAL television system and converted them so they would work in South Africa. Sony, the proprietor of the SONY mark registered under the 1963 Act, and its registered user Tedelex obtained an interdict in the court below. Television appealed.

## Held (verbatim extracts, SAFLII text checked 2026-11-10)

On the statute:

> "The parties' contentions in this regard require a consideration of sec. 44(1) of the Act."

On the principle:

> "If another person alters the goods, they are, to the extent to which they have been altered, no longer the goods to which the trade mark was affixed by the proprietor. It then, it seems to me, becomes a matter of degree whether there has been a change in the goods sufficiently appreciable to render them no longer the "genuine goods", i.e., the goods which the trade mark proprietor had marked."

On the outcome:

> "It follows therefore, that the recorders sold by Television are not the "genuine goods" which were marked by Sony with its trade mark. The sale of such recorders bearing Sony's trade mark constitutes, in my view, an infringement pursuant to sec.44(l)(a) of the Act."

> "In the result the appeal is dismissed with costs"

(The SAFLII text has scanning artefacts. Words split by a hyphen — "mat-ter", "re-corders" — are joined in the extracts above; "44(l)" is left as SAFLII prints it, for "44(1)".)

## Operative principle

Resale protection covers genuine goods as the proprietor marked them. Material alteration after marking — a change of specification, a conversion of the underlying product — can take the goods outside that protection, and whether the change is enough "becomes a matter of degree".

## Citation note

Television Radio Centre was the **appellant** and Sony the **respondent**; the appeal was dismissed, so Sony's infringement claim stood. Commentary sometimes reverses the parties.
