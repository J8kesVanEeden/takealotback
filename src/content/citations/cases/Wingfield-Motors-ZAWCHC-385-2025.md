# Wingfield Motors (Pty) Ltd t/a Best Price For My Car v National Consumer Tribunal and Others
## [2025] ZAWCHC 385; 2026 (2) SA 662 (WCC) | Western Cape Division | 26 August 2025

Judgment by Nuku J and O'Brien AJ. An appeal/review by a used-car dealer against a National Consumer Tribunal finding. Reported in the South African Law Reports at 2026 (2) SA 662 (WCC).

**Citation note:** SAFLII lists the judgment as [2025] ZAWCHC 385 (its URL and title), but the judgment's own "Neutral citation" line reads "[2025] ZAWCHC 381 (26-08-2025)". The site cites 385 (the SAFLII listing) together with the law-report reference, which is unambiguous.

## Proposition cited on the site

Where the evidence points to a defect, **a supplier that blames the consumer's use has to prove it**; speculation is not evidence. Section 56 applies to all goods without exception.

## Verbatim extracts (SAFLII, opened 2026-11-10)

> [89] "To the extent that Wingfield claims that the defects in the clutch and the flywheel were caused by the manner of driving of the Customer or his son, Wingfield did not present any evidence to support that claim but merely speculated. With the evidence pointing to the presence of the defects, it was for Wingfield to prove that vehicle goods had been altered after leaving its control. …"

> [87] "… Wingfield, under section 56(2)(a) of the CPA, was required to cover the costs of repairs. The provisions of section 56 are explicit; they apply to all goods without exception, and Wingfield's attempt to argue that section 56 does not apply to used goods is misplaced."

## Scope and limits

Vehicle case. A Western Cape Division decision: Takealot's own T&Cs choose the Western Cape High Court as forum, which makes this Division's approach directly relevant to any High Court dispute with Takealot. The court distinguished *Motus v Wentzel* (para 86) because the defects there "required more investigation as they were not immediately obvious".
