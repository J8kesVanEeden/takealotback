# Knowler: Scholle stolen laptop (TimesLive)
## TimesLive (Sunday Times Daily), 06 November 2022 · Wendy Knowler

## Why the site cites this

This is Knowler's own writing on the Scholle case, companion to the MyBroadband article. Adds Stephanie Scholle's (Azel's wife) own words, setting her grievance against Takealot's procedural refusal, and Knowler's own concluding observation. Different publication, same facts: the corroboration matters.

## Extracts (fair dealing for criticism and review)

From Wendy Knowler, "How committed to customer relations are retailers?", TimesLive (Sunday Times Daily), 6 November 2022. Quoted under s 12(1)(b) of the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, with the author and source named. Trimmed on 2026-11-23 to the passages the site relies on.

(MyBroadband reported R14,000 and "Axel"; Knowler reports R14,400 and "Azel". The underlying case is the same.)

### The purchase

> "Husband Azel bought a Hewlett Packard laptop on the platform in November 2019, paying R14,400."

### The flip-flop (Knowler's account)

> "Then things took a bad turn. Takealot reversed its decision, saying there would be no refund. In an email the couple was told: 'Please be advised the return was declined due to tampering, as the unit shows signs of being opened.'"

### Stephanie Scholle's rebuttal

> "The main problem with the laptop is that it is a stolen unit that was sold to us as new via the Takealot platform nearly three years ago. The laptop is upgradeable — that is not tampering."

### Knowler's concluding observation

> "And yet when the Scholles alerted the company in October — two months after the suspension — that they had just discovered the laptop bought from that supplier via Takealot three years earlier was stolen, they were refused recourse because of a perfectly legitimate upgrade. Despite that they were not only sold used goods but stolen goods, and despite that the laptop, three years on, had been remotely disabled by the organisation it had been stolen from, rendering it useless."

## Site reliance

- **Clause 15 (marketplace sellers) corroborating angle:** confirms the MyBroadband narrative. The two sources together (independent SA tech outlet and columnist on Arena Holdings) strengthen the citation.
- **Stephanie Scholle calls it "a stolen unit that was sold to us as new".** That is the consumer-side grievance in her own words: the supplier's vetting failure is the *primary* fault, and the refusal over "tampering" is downstream noise.
