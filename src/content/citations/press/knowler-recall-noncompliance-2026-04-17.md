# Wendy Knowler | Exploding power banks, risky antacids, and a shrug from Takealot
## News24, 17/18 April 2026 · Wendy Knowler

## Why the site cites this

The article reports complaints about how Takealot handled two recalls:

1. **ESR HaloLock Kickstand wireless power bank, Model 2G505B**: global recall, listed among the product recalls announced by the National Consumer Commission in 2026 (the NCC's own notice, 8 April 2026, describes the recall as notified by the supplier, not as an NCC order; see Site reliance below). Two named consumers (Ben Hart, Mahendren Chetty) bought the unit through Takealot in Oct/Nov 2024. Both were refused refunds when they sought them post-recall: Takealot told Chetty the product's 12-month warranty had expired, and gave Hart a final response that it could not confirm the recall (extracts below). Takealot's PR agency declined to respond to Knowler's query.
2. **Citro-Soda Regular**: SAHPRA recall (foreign-material contamination: metal fragments). Monogran Naidoo notified by Takealot, product collected, but the refund issued was a coupon valid for **two days** that expired in his spam folder before he saw it. Takealot's explanation: Naidoo had paid with a promotional coupon, so it reissued that coupon on its original terms, including the short validity; after Knowler's query it credited him the coupon's full value (extract below).

These cases ground the site's clause on recalls: a recall is strong evidence the goods failed CPA s 55. Within six months of delivery s 56 gives the return-and-refund election. Beyond that, the recall notice's own refund instruction is the route, with s 61 if the product caused harm, and warranty expiry is not an answer to a recall refund. Note the ESR buyers here bought in October/November 2024, so by the April 2026 recall s 56's six-month window had closed for them.

(Corrected 2026-11-23: the title and summary called these "recall non-compliance cases against Takealot". They are complaints, which Knowler referred to the NCC; no NCC finding is recorded, and Takealot's coupon explanation is now included.)

## Extracts (fair dealing for criticism and review)

From Wendy Knowler, Consumer Lookout, News24, 17 April 2026. Quoted under s 12(1)(b) of the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, with the author and source named. Trimmed on 2026-11-23 to the passages the site relies on.

### The recall notice

> "Consumers in possession of the affected products are urged to immediately stop using them and return them to a point of purchase for a refund." (NCC recall notice)

### Hart's case

Ben Hart bought the unit in October 2024 for around R1,000. About a year later (weeks after manufacturer warranty expired) it stopped charging properly and felt hot. He discovered the global recall, then emailed Takealot, saying he understood the CPA required it to respect the recall:

> "But after much back and forth, Takealot ultimately chose to ignore this. Their final response was that they could not confirm the recall and therefore absolved themselves of any responsibility."

(Hart was refunded by ESR directly after engaging the manufacturer.)

> "I pointed out again to Takealot that if anyone on their side had merely emailed the manufacturer, they could have received the same response, but they still stonewalled me."

### Chetty's case

> "But Takealot told him: 'There is a 12-month warranty on the product, which expired on 12 Nov 2025 ... The item is well out of warranty.'"

### The Citro-Soda case (Naidoo)

> "Notably, it was valid for just two days and had expired before he became aware of it, leaving him effectively without the product and without compensation."

### Takealot's eventual remediation (Citro-Soda only)

> "Following the recall announcement, Takealot engaged all affected customers as per our standard recall procedures, offering a refund in the form of the original payment method. In Mr Naidoo's case, full payment for the recalled product was made via a promotional coupon. As per our refund and coupons policy, we reissued the original coupon aligned to the original coupon terms and conditions, including the limited time validity period."
>
> "As a gesture of goodwill, we have now credited Mr Naidoo the full value of the original coupon, not just the value of the recalled product."

### Referral to the NCC

> "I have reported Takealot's recall responses to the NCC and await its response."

## Site reliance

Rewritten 2026-11-10 after the attribution audit (an internal review of every source attribution on the site). The earlier version leaned on CPA s 19, s 58, "Chapter 3" and an "after investigation / unreasonable risk of harm" reading of s 60; none of those survive a check against the Act.

- **The recall notice**: the NCC notice quoted above tells consumers to "return them to a point of purchase for a refund". That instruction is the consumer's main route once s 56's six months have passed. The NCC's own ESR notice (thencc.org.za/product-safety-recall-esr-halolock-wireless-power-banks/, dated 8 April 2026, read by us on 2026-11-10) describes the recall as notified by the supplier, not as an NCC order under s 60(2).
- **CPA s 60(2)**: the NCC can, on reasonable grounds to believe goods may be unsafe, require the producer by written notice to investigate or "carry out a recall programme on any terms required by the Commission". It is addressed to the producer or importer.
- **CPA s 55 and s 56**: a recall is strong evidence the goods were not safe, good-quality goods (s 55). The s 56 election runs only "within six months after the delivery".
- **CPA s 61**: strict liability for harm caused by unsafe goods; joint and several where more than one person is liable (s 61(3)).
- **CPA s 41**: a listing that still presents a recalled product as safe is arguably a misleading representation. (s 58 covers risky activities/facilities and hazardous-goods packaging, not listings.)
- **NCC enforcement**: compliance notice (s 100); administrative fine by the Tribunal (s 112); failing to comply with a compliance notice is an offence (s 110(2)). s 60 sits in Chapter 2 of the Act.
- **Refund mechanics**: Takealot's statement on the Citro-Soda recall ("engaged all affected customers as per our standard recall procedures, offering a refund in the form of the original payment method") is a statement to Knowler, not a published procedure. The coupon Naidoo received was "valid for just two days". The article does not say whether those were calendar or business days.
